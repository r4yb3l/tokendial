Tokendial colours every dial the same way: **green under 50 %**, **amber from 50 to 80 %**, **red from 80 %**. The red band starts earlier than the usual 70 % on purpose, so the colour change and the 80 % alert land at the same moment. Here is what each band is asking you to do.

## Green: spend freely

Under half the window, the cheapest thing you can do is not think about it. Start the big task, let the agent read the whole repository, run the long test suite. This is what the window is for.

## Amber: finish, do not start

Between 50 and 80 % the question changes from "can I?" to "should I start something new here?". The honest answer is usually no. Finish the thought you are in, land the change, commit. Do not open a fresh, exploratory conversation on a window that is already half spent.

The card tells you how long until the reset. If it is under an hour, a coffee is cheaper than a wasted prompt.

## Red: switch or wait

At 80 % Tokendial sends the alert. Two moves make sense:

1. **Move the task to another assistant.** If Codex or Copilot is green, hand it the next step. Most vibecoders run two or three subscriptions precisely for this; the dial is what makes the hand-off obvious instead of a guess.
2. **Wait for the reset.** If the reset is close, the "reset soon" alert tells you ten minutes before it comes back, and again when a limit lifts. Go stretch.

What does not make sense is pushing through. The last 20 % of a window is the most expensive part: every prompt you spend there is one you will not have when the agent is halfway through a change and stops with a wall.

## Change the numbers if your rhythm is different

The thresholds are yours to set. Someone who works in long single sessions may want 60, 85 and 95; someone who bounces between tools all day may prefer an early 40 % nudge. Open Settings, edit the list, done. The bands on the dial follow.
